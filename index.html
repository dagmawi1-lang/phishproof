Paste a suspicious email or text and see the red flags, explained. It runs on your device, never opens links, and sends nothing anywhere.

Tip: in Gmail use ⋮ → "Show original", or just copy the sender line and the text.

    What to do

    • Don't click links or open attachments in a message you aren't sure about.
    • Go to the company's website by typing its address yourself, or use its app.
    • If it claims to be someone you know, check with them another way (text, call, in person).
    • Report it: use "Report phishing" in your email app, or forward texts to 7726 (SPAM).

    How it works
    
    
    PhishProof checks for 19 warning signs that security teams teach: lookalike domains ("paypa1", "rnicrosoft"), links whose text hides a different destination, bare IP links, shortened links, punycode, mismatched sender names and reply-to addresses, urgent pressure, requests for passwords or gift cards, and disguised attachments like .pdf.exe.
    
    
    No tool catches every scam. If something feels off, it probably is, so verify it another way.
